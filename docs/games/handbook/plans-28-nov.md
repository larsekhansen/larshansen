## The theme, written down

Your words: Oslo "quaint, Nordic-national-romantic, less technological, less crowded, calm", against bad guys who are "glam, neon, lights, American, loud". And Loki: "He is the god of mischief. And why would he really like the nordics anyway, they fucked him over."

| --- | --- | --- |
| Look | Wood, stone, wool, snow, water. Muted colours, soft light. Few people, no screens. | Neon, chrome, sequins, spotlights. Everything lit and moving. |
| Manner | Few words, dry, understated. Nobody boasts. A nod is praise. | Many words, all superlatives. Everything is the greatest show on earth. |

**The Norwegian trope to push:** understatement. Where the Dutch are direct, Norwegians say less than they mean: "ikke verst" (not bad) for something wonderful, a long pause, getting on with it, going for a walk whatever the weather. The hero answers Loki's speeches with three words.

**Loki's reason:** mischief, and a grudge. The gods bound him under the earth; he owes the North nothing. A rich loud man who also felt spurned is the perfect patron. He does it for the fun and for the revenge, and the money is a joke to him.

## Plan 1: the city as real Oslo

Your words: "I just want to walk to actual streets as they are and see actual parts of the city. I want real station names and I want the walk in between the station and the goal to be the actual road traveled. Like between Tøyen and Vålerenga."

### The idea

- **Every level is one real walk**: out of a real station, along the real streets in their real order, to the real goal. What you pass on the way is the level: its landmarks become its platforms, its set pieces and its jokes.

- **The map becomes the real network, simplified**: the shared tunnel through the centre with its branches, plus the tram up to Ekeberg and the boat to Bygdøy. Real station names on the boards.

- **A station can have more than one exit.** Jernbanetorget opens the Opera walk and the Bjørvika walk; Stortinget opens the fortress walk and Karl Johan. That is true to the city and solves three levels sharing a station.

- **Length follows the walk.** A short walk (Nationaltheatret to Rådhuset, 400 m) is a short level; a long one (down Akerselva) is long or is cut at a real place. Rough scale: a screen is 40 to 60 m of street, compressed between landmarks, never reordered.

- Everything stays in the theme above: the real streets drawn calm and a little older than today, with Loki's neon laid over them.

### How I would do it

- **You correct the walks.** For each row: is this the way you would walk it, and what would you never leave out? Ten minutes with this table. I then check every route on a proper map before any drawing.

- **The map and the names** (1 round). The real network with exits, real station names in the bar and on the boards. No level changes yet, so it is cheap to judge and to undo.

- **The pilot: Tøyen to Vålerenga** (2 rounds). One level rebuilt fully as the real walk, so you can play it and say whether this is the feeling you want before the rest follows.

- **The large and medium ones** (4 to 6 rounds): Akerselva, Akershus, Holmenkollen, Bygdøy, Bjørvika, Gamlebyen.

- **The small ones** (2 to 3 rounds): the rest.

### What it costs and risks

- **Time:** 9 to 12 rounds. Each rebuilt level loses its tuned jumps and gets new ones, so each needs its checks again.

- **Nobody has played yet.** Rebuilding levels before you have played the current ones means the current tuning is never judged. I would like you to play at least the pilot before step 4.

- **The runes** are tied to the count of levels (ten and six). If Akerselva splits, the count changes. See plan 3.

- **Privacy stays as it is:** public streets and landmarks, no address, no private house singled out.
