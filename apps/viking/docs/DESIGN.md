# Vikingfjorden: how it is built

A side-view game built the way the front page is built. The reasons (one cloth for all three games, the style bible) are in [`docs/games/PLAN.md`](../../../docs/games/PLAN.md), the levels with a picture of each in [`docs/games/LEVELS.html`](../../../docs/games/LEVELS.html), line 2's plan in [`docs/games/PLAN-LINE-2.md`](../../../docs/games/PLAN-LINE-2.md); this file says what is here.

The kit (`packages/pixelkit`) holds the map reader, the physics with moving ground, surfaces (ice, an inrun, a belt) and zones that push (`zone.ts`, on a beat for a gust), hazards (`hazard.ts`), the dash, carriers on a path (stopping at stations), an orbit or a lift, flags, cannons and drums, ledges on a beat and pulses (`timed.ts`), the dark with lights through it (`dark.ts`, which the maze's dark level uses too), the camera, doors, props and the scene helpers; and line 2's pieces: the rocking deck (`deck.ts`), the sinking pad (`sink.ts`) and the oar bank (`oars.ts`); and what is drawn once and copied (`kept.ts`, ADR 0036, 0041: `kept` for a piece in its place, `keptStrips` for a large one in 128 px strips, `stamp` for a shape alike wherever it stands, `seen` for whether a piece of the street is on the screen at all; a chunk of its own, as `dark.ts` is). Swatches used by one level alone (`swatches/metro.ts`, `hill.ts`, `winter.ts`, `museum.ts`, `park.ts`, `sledge.ts`, `lake.ts`, `site.ts`, `ruins.ts`) are imported by that level directly, so they come in its file.

## The rules

- **Movement**: the kit's physics with the front page's constants, 60 steps a second: run 1.6, a jump 46 px high and 54 px far; the jump still works ten steps (0.17 s) after walking off an edge, and a jump pressed six steps before landing still happens (ADR 0048: from six to ten in order 23, no route made harder). The **raven's feather** from the maze gives a second jump in the air (91 px high, 101 px far): on when `localStorage["viking.feather"] === "1"`, or with `?feather`. It is read again when he comes out of the mound. With `viking.featherGold === "1"` the pompom and the feather pixels are gold. `?feather` and `?pole` work without `?debug`, deliberately: anyone may try the game with the abilities (and keep a bracteate found so); it is a personal site, not a contest.
- **A level** starts at `S` and ends at its rune: the clock stops, the rune flies to its slot in the header, and a card shows the time, the best, the skilling, the bracteate and what is new. It has one action, "Til kartet" / "To the map": any key newly pressed, a tap or a click takes him there, and after six seconds it goes by itself (a thin line fills under the button; not under reduced motion, where it waits). He arrives at the station he cleared, the next one open ahead of him. Playing a level again is what the map is for; R in a level still restarts it. The Game over card keeps "Try again" (ADR 0037).
- **The header's runes** (order 18): a rune he has is a solid gold tile with the rune dark on it and a tick at its corner; one he has not is an empty slot, a faint dashed outline with the rune barely there; the level he is in is ringed. 27 x 33 on a desktop (21 x 30 with sixteen); on an upright phone the four links share one row, so the runes have a row of their own (30 x 34, sixteen 19 x 28) and the clock, hearts and the rest a row under them, all in view; with sixteen below 1400 px the best time is left to the card. The line map's rune stones speak the same language: gold with a tick once cleared, an empty dashed slot until then, both readable after dark.
- **Hearts and lives**: three hearts; an enemy's touch, something thrown, or a fall into water or a pit takes one, knocks him back and leaves him blinking and safe for a second and a half (a pale figure every other twelfth of a second, which reads after dark as well; with reduced motion a still pale outline instead). A fall while he still blinks from a hit puts him back but takes no second heart (ADR 0018). Falls put him back at the last flag or bench (or the last firm ground where there is none yet). No hearts left: a life goes and he starts at the last flag or bench with full hearts. No lives left: a plain Game over card, and the level starts over with three lives; nothing cleared or found is lost. In a boss's fight (the boss awake) Game over goes on instead from the bench before it, with three lives and the boss whole and asleep again; the card says so (ADR 0017); and a single lost life there never puts him further back than that bench (ADR 0033). Any way back into a level after a Game over (the card, R, the header's restart, Back, a link) gives three lives. Lives and skilling go with him from level to level during a visit; 100 skilling or a slice of brown cheese is a life. A visit remembers, per level, that the cheese was taken and which skilling and beaten enemies have paid into the purse, so restarting a level farms nothing (ADR 0018).
- **Benches**: up at a bench to sit; the hearts fill and the bench is where falls bring him back.
- **Enemies** (in every level; how they look, see "Colour says what a thing is" below; since order 18 a quarter larger, their boxes grown with them on Holmenkollen, Nordmarka, Ekeberg, Vålerenga, Bygdøy, Vigelandsparken, Korketrekkeren and Østensjøvannet and kept on the other eight, where growing them cost the sloppy player hearts, ADR 0039): walkers, flyers, throwers and jumpers are beaten by landing on them (higher with the jump held); spiky things hurt from above too; throwers lob on a beat after a one-second wind-up, and only while he is within seven tiles; jumpers leap out of water after a ripple. A beaten enemy's glamour breaks (order 27, "The glamour" below: its glammed self flashed, its marks flying off, then its natural self in the calm outline, leaving; nobody dies in this game) and gives a skilling. Langkaia's pigeon on the quay is the first, on its own; Akershus's brazier on the ground before the outer wall is the first spiky thing, Rådhuset's chip-throwing gull the first thrower. A thrower may face one way only (`faces`: Bygdøy's slingers on the storehouses' ridges look out over the yard and throw only at him in front of them, not right under them) and may lead him (`lead`: it aims where he is going when he comes at it, and allows for the drop from above him): walking on under it is what it punishes, and the shield is the answer. Where they stand: no patrol within three tiles of a flag or a bench, no thrower within reach of a flag, a bench or a sign (tested); no patrol within three tiles of where a route's jump over water or a pit takes off or lands, whatever the move is called, and no flyer whose wave crosses the jump's arc (tested from the routes since order 13, the flyers and every move since order 17, with named exceptions: in Nabolaget the snowball kid at the edge of the block's roof and the cat at the foot of the drift, both stomped by the route first; in Østensjøvannet the three swans, whose water the pads lie on and which cannot be touched until he has lingered there; four of line 1's flyers, measured to cost the sloppy player little there); none by a drop, and nothing spiky on or beside ice (checked by the sloppy player, not by a test). After dusk (any sky but the day's) every outline keeps its light (the warning red round what can hurt, the calm pale round what cannot: order 22), each enemy and what it throws gets a little of its own light back through the night's tint, with eye-shine where it has eyes (the pixels it drew in `ENEMY.eye`); the hazards' warning stripe and the paint on what he stands on keep their light, and the skilling shine nearly as by day.
- **Surfaces**: ice has little grip, and the red rails stop a slide; an inrun has none at all, draws him on and lets his speed climb past a run; leaving the ground faster than a run keeps the speed in the air.
- **The shield** (given at Bygdøy's prow; ADR 0024): holding down on the ground raises it, facing the way he faces, in six steps, and it stays up as long as down is held. While it comes up and is up he cannot walk, jump or dash (a jump pressed then is kept a tenth of a second and made as down is let go, ADR 0032); he can turn, and it comes up again the other way. Up, it stops a throw coming at him from in front (faster across than 0.3 px a step towards him; it falls, harmless) and holds off a walker, a spiky thing, a flyer or a boss that runs into him from in front at his height, pushed back a tile (the plough then backs off to rest with the round's hint; the troll steps back and raises its club there, ADR 0032). It does not stop a fall, a stone dropping from above, anything from below (a jumper, the troll's wave), the troll's club, the third rail, or anything from behind. From the level after Bygdøy on he always has it, and everywhere once Bygdøy is cleared. `?shield` gives it for checking, as `?pole` does. On a touch screen the Shield button beside Dash, once he has it (with both, the pad's buttons are smaller). Down meant nothing in a level before it; up still sits at a bench. At Bygdøy's passage, where it is first asked for, its key shows over him ("↓ Shield") and the gift's bubble ends there. With reduced motion it is drawn down until it protects and then up.
- **The ski pole** (from clearing Holmenkollen): a dash of three tiles in ten steps, level, once each time in the air, and on the ground again after a short rest (24 steps). From Akerselva on he always has it, and some gaps need it. On a touch screen the Dash button; the sign and the card that teach it name the button there.
- **Zones** (Akerselva's falls): a rectangle that pushes him while he is in it. A waterfall pushes down: under it a jump barely leaves the ground, and walking off an edge into one drops him in its pool. Jump over a fall's top, or dash through it: the dash ignores the push.
- **The dark** (Nordmarka): under the spruces only his headlamp's circle shows, a little round him and more the way he faces; lit windows, the blue trail marks, the sign boards, the bogs' water and the dawn show through, and eyes shine in it. The marsh and the clearings are lighter. The headlamp lights what stands in front of the backdrop (trees, walls, the ground's top edge); where its circle falls on the sky, the far layers or deeper into the soil, the night is put back.
- **The troll** (Nordmarka's end, with a flag and a bench right before): asleep it is a boulder; it wakes when he comes in, walks at him, raises its club for a full second, slams it (a wave runs along the ground: jump it), and the club sticks for three seconds. Stuck where the dawn comes through the trees (a bright shaft with a pool of light on the floor, pulsing while the club is stuck) with his headlamp on it (seven tiles), a third of it turns to stone, the wave stops and it backs off, quickly, out of the light; three times, and it is a boulder with rune N on it. Its lines show in a bubble beside it, on the side away from him. A round with no hit shows a two-line hint over it and sends it back to the far end to come again; so does ten seconds of not being able to get nearer him. Its body hurts only while it walks or raises the club, not while the club is stuck, it flinches or it backs off. A lost life sends it back to sleep, its stone kept; Game over in the fight, see Hearts.
- **Flags** (checkpoints): touched once, the front page's flag goes up the pole, and falls bring him back there. Forwards only.
- **Moving ground** (carriers): rafts that bob or drift (two on Langkaia drift together and stand side by side), a rowing boat moored against the longship's stern (solid, so a step too far bumps into the hull), a ferry that crosses, lifts, a hoist, a bell that swings, banners on a path, logs that drift, lie a second against the rock at the end of their stretch and come round again, buckets on a water wheel and the tips of Rådhuset's clock hand (orbits), a moose's back. He is carried with them, and drawn on the same whole pixels as they are.
- **Cannons**: walk in, a third of a second in the barrel (the arc is shown, dotted), then the shot. In the air the keys only lean on the flight. A cannon he lands in takes him in: Akershus's saluting battery fires him from one to the next.
- **Ledges on a beat** (the bells): two seconds there, one second of the maze's warning shimmer, two gone. Neighbours are a step apart. Bygdøy's oars are ledges on a beat too, drawn as oars out of their ports (`oars.ts`), each a stroke behind the one below; an oar rings no bell (`note: -1`).
- **Rocking decks** (Bygdøy; `deck.ts`): a deck that tilts on a beat between two angles, a row of narrow ledges (4 px) that rise and fall in whole pixels, the ends most. Standing on it he is carried up and down with the ledge under his middle and slides nowhere; walking along it he is carried the pixel or two from one ledge to the next. Fram's far end must be up for a jump to reach her foredeck.
- **Sinking pads** (Bygdøy's bundles of balsa, Østensjøvannet's lily pads; `sink.ts`): firm for 40 steps under him (a lily pad 100: ADR 0029), then under over 30 (and he with it, into the water), back 90 steps after he leaves; stepping off before it sinks, it forgets him. The reach search takes every height a deck's ledge or a pad ever has, as it takes a log's.
- **A level's own rules** (`LevelDef.rules`, in the level's file, run by `play.ts` each step as it runs the troll's): its own enemies (by their look), ground of its own, the keys he is left with, an after-step hook, and for the reach search what carries him and what ground can ever be there; for the camera, how far it looks ahead on the sledge.

## The glamour

Order 27 (ADR 0050). Lars, on a page of every redrawn figure: "hm. all the pink... im just not sold on pink being a bad guy
color. but maybe if we make it a theme somehow? like, oh no, the 80s glam invaders are here!" The Neon Invaders have put a
glamour (the old word: a spell that makes a thing appear other than it is) on Oslo. Everything under it is glammed up in
neon and out to hurt him; a stomp breaks it and the creature is itself again and leaves; a rune lifts it from a station.
**Natural is Oslo, neon is the glamour**: that is the game's reading at a glance, on top of the outlines.

- **One gang by treatment, not by hue.** Bodies are the glamour's family (`ROLES.glamour`): the enemy palette's warm end (its
  plum is the magenta, its orange the laser orange, its poison the acid green) and the rest (hot pink, magenta, purple and its
  dark, electric blue, lime). Every glammed frame gets a sheen of two neons along its lit top, the first over its front half,
  the second over the quarter behind (`sheened`). After dark each invader glows in its commonest neon under its outline
  (`glows`, made once a shape). Each level leans its two neons its own way (`LEANS`: Nordmarka acid green and purple,
  Bjørvika electric blue and hot pink, the plum ramp hot pink there too).
- **Marks drawn into the maps**: a few per figure, pieces marked `glam` (`mark(rows, dx, dy)` in `alive/foes.ts`, in the
  letters `N` and `n`, which are the level's two neons, the near-black and the eye): shades (`SHADES`, side on: the arm, the
  lens, the glint, which is the eye's pixel and still shines after dark), a crest, shoulder pads, a headband, leg warmers, a
  collar, a bolt, neon tubes. A frame with marks has its bare self and its marks (`Frame.bare`, `Frame.marks`).
- **Its natural self** is the same map without its marks in a ramp of its real colours (`NATURAL` in `alive/natural.ts`),
  with how it leaves. A stomp: its glammed self flashed, its marks flying off, glitter in its level's neons, then its natural
  self in the calm outline leaving its way, fading from step 30, gone at 48 (`drawBreak`); with reduced motion a plain change
  of colour. The rune: a ring of daylight runs out from the stone, and every invader it passes is drawn as its natural self in
  the calm outline (`feel`'s `lifted`). Drawing only, both.
- **Fire that hurts burns neon** (`glamFire`: magenta with lime tongues and tips), and its natural self an ordinary orange.
  Fires and lamps that are scenery stay warm.
- **The bosses are headliners**: the troll's moss gone magenta in a mullet and a spiked wristband, the plough a tour bus with a
  two-neon stripe and stage lights, the serpent a neon crest and shades; each loses its glam in its last frames. Every tell is
  where it was, and no glam is the "now" violet.
- **Harmless stays natural**: what moves and cannot hurt him (sheep, the moose, the heron, the parade) uses no neon.
- **Loki, the front man** (order 29, ADR 0052), is met once, at the encore: under the glamour all of him is the enemy palette (pink and
  magenta hair, wraparound shades, an acid-green jacket with white pads, spandex, white boots, stage make-up); each hit knocks a piece off,
  and plain he is natural colours held off every neon and role. The stage's neon, beams and mirror ball are his glamour and go plain with him.

**Adding an enemy** (or a level with one): draw it from the enemy palette as frames, as before; give it one or two glam marks
as `mark` pieces that suit the animal (never more than two pixels over the head he lands on: the palette test; never a
quarter of it in the purple, the magenta or the electric blue, which sit near a role: the palette test names them); give it an
entry in `NATURAL` (every enemy colour it is drawn in, to its real one; how it leaves): `alive/natural.test.ts` holds that it
has one, that it is never neon and that its ending leaves. A look not drawn through `drawFoe` honours `glam.mode` itself
(`glamFrame`, as the statues and the salmon do). A new level gets a lean in `LEANS` (the family only) and two to four pieces of
evidence in `PLACES` (`tools/glance/invasion.ts` finds where; `invasion.test.ts` holds it). Its words, if the story has any for
it, go in `game/story.ts`, both languages.

   and the small things every level has; `tools/review/o25/old-style.md` says what is left and why: scenery.) A figure or a
   small thing is a per-pixel map (`frame` in `src/alive/frames.ts`: rows of letters, each letter a colour), with a ramp
   of three to five tones per material, a lit side and a shaded side, eyes. Its tones come from its role's colours (the
   enemy palette's ramps: plum light, plum, plum dark, the near-black; white and its shade; orange, green and pink from
   their light to the near-black), held by `render/palette.test.ts`.
   A look drawn as frames is drawn through `parts` with no bob (`drawFoe`), so a kept box still squashes it to its height;
   one drawn for the box its level keeps says so (`drawFoe(…, fit)`, order 25) and is not squashed, so no row is lost.
2. **Every new enemy has**: an idle of two frames or more (a breath on its own clock; stone that is watched has one); a
   move cycle of three or four, a walker's by the distance it covers (three pixels a frame, so its feet hold), a flyer's
   by its clock; a wind-up pose if it throws, changing its outline by 18 pixels or more (order 19); and its death is the
   page's (three frames from its own shape). A look still drawn in rectangles gets the cycle and the breath from `parts`
   and `stride`; `alive/frames.test.ts` holds every look of every level to it, and that no frame leaves its look's box.
3. **What moves casts a shadow** on the ground under it (`alive/shadows.ts`): nothing to do for an enemy, a sheep, a shot
   or a carrier; a level's own mover that is not one of these draws none.
4. **Frames are picked from what the rules keep**, never the other way: no box, speed or timing changes for a picture.
5. **Reduced motion** keeps idles and cycles (they say what a thing is doing), and drops squash, stretch and shaking.
6. **Depth**: a band of water or snow behind the lane is a receding plane (`plane` in `alive/depth.ts`); a far layer's
   pieces may go at a speed of their own (`spedX`), the farther ones first; aim for five speeds between the sky and the lane
   by splitting what the layer has, not by drawing more. Something in front of him (`alive/fore.ts`) only where
   `fore.test.ts` allows it (nothing he must read in the six tiles ahead as it passes), at most four a level. Since order
   24 each is a shape a person knows at a glance (a mast with its yard and furled sail, a lamp post with its lantern, a trunk
   with bark and roots), drawn a half again larger than the street (it is nearer), in a dark tone of the level's own air,
   never over the banner (clipped out), and gone (faded in a sixth of a second) while a bubble is up or he sits, so it never
   stands over him or what he reads; its height is measured from under the page's bar, which the canvases run on up behind.
7. **Things that live in a place** (order 24): water laps at a quay's wall in three frames (`lap`, drawn once and copied,
   through a hook in `drawWater`); a torch's or a fire basket's flame flickers in three (`flame`), an oil lamp gutters now
   and then (`lampFlicker`), all still with reduced motion; the flag the game flies is its own (the kit's is the front
   page's), three frames of a ripple at a pace set by its level's wind (`WIND` in `alive/things.ts`), hanging in still air
   under Stortinget. Smoke from chimneys and birds far off go where a sky was empty, quieter than everything he plays with.

## The ground under him

Order 26 (ADR 0049). Everything he stands on and walks past (ground, walls, platforms' bodies, crates, stairs, the props at
arm's length) is drawn from one textured set, `src/alive/ground.ts`. For anyone adding a surface or a level:

1. **Name a material, do not draw a tile.** A level's surface is a `Grain` in the level's file: its name, its `kind`
   (blocks for stone in courses, brick, setts and slabs; planks; earth under a top band of grass, sand, paving, gravel,
   moss, setts, snow or reed; rock in facets; snow in layers; ice; steel; concrete; a crate; a girder), its ramp of three
   to five tones of one hue, darkest first (the joint, the shade, the face, the light, a highlight), and what the kind
   takes (a course's height and a block's width, a kerb's rows for its top, a top band's ramp, accents: moss, salt, a
   tuft, a flower, rivets, a sparkle, a pebble's lit top). Take the tones from what the level already paints for that
   surface; mix between them rather than toward white or black.
2. **Draw its tiles with `tile(grain, c, r, x, y, map, sky)`** from the level's `art.tile`. The edges come from the map
   (`edgesAt`: the sky over its top, open ends, a different thing below, an overhang, a step beside its top, snow, the
   water's line beside it); a level whose idea of "open" differs passes its own (`edgesAt(map, c, r, sky) & ~EDGE.WET`, or
   bits of `EDGE`). Depth (0 to 3) darkens nothing by itself: a level that darkens deeper down makes a grain a step.
   What a tile has that is not its material (a yellow line along a platform, warning stripes on a beam, rebar, a cross
   cut in a gravestone) is drawn over the piece as before.
3. **The shared letters** (`#` with the level's `ground(c)` and its `kind`, the bed under water, `Q`, `M`, `W`, `X`, `P`,
   `K`, `B`) need nothing: the set draws them. One-way ledges keep the kit's drawing and paint; the set textures their
   body under the paint.
4. **The paint sits on top exactly as now** (ADR 0044): nothing in a material is the paint's yellow, and nothing in it looks
   standable where it is not.
5. **The band.** Every material: three to five tones, of one hue (40 degrees at most between the hues of its coloured tones),
   its darkest to its lightest at most 40 (CIEDE2000) and below the figures' own contrast (38.7); and no new tone of a level's
   ground nearer any role than that level's ground was before order 26 (`render/palette.test.ts`). Where a tone fails, use one
   the level already paints.
6. **Every solid tile is the set's**, or named with its reason in `alive/ground.test.ts` (a thing, not ground: a boat, a
   rail, a lantern; a house drawn by the back art; what is drawn from the play's state).
7. **Cheap.** A piece is painted once and copied: a tile is one copy. A prop of its own size (a sign board, a door) is a
   piece of its own, kept by its size. Never draw a texture per frame in rectangles.

`tools/glance/groundsheet.ts` draws every level's every surface outside the page (by day or under its own sky) and
`groundsheet.py` lays them out before and after at any scale; `tools/glance/ground.ts` measures each level's ground against
the roles and the figures.

## Props at arm's length

Order 28 (ADR 0051). What stands in the lane and is not ground, an enemy or a pickup (lamp posts, railings, walls of planks,
doors, posts, ladders, boats, carriers' bodies) is drawn from the props kit, `src/alive/props.ts`, as the ground is drawn from
the textured set. For anyone adding a prop to a level:

1. **Ask the three questions first.** What does he do with it: if he stands on it, its top is where the physics has it and the
   page paints it yellow (draw nothing that competes: no lit top of your own); if he uses it, it is a door, a bench or a sign and
   the page marks its corners in cyan; if it hurts, it is a hazard or an enemy and is outlined in red. A prop that is none of
   these must not look like one: no flat lit top over a shadow line (a railing's rail is a line with a glint, not a ledge),
   nothing in a coin's amber or a rune's gold, nothing shaped like a door that is not one. Does it fit its level: it is of its
   place, as Oslo has it (the old town's cast iron, the square's lamp, the harbour's steel, the park's wrought iron, tarred
   timber on the fjord). Should the glamour touch it: no. Props are Oslo; neon is the invaders' (ADR 0050).
2. **Take it from the kit where the kit has it**: `lamp(kind, x, ground, tall, lit)` (`old`, `square`, `harbour`, `park`; the
   glass is registered as a lamp's light, so it comes on after dark), `railing(kind, x0, x1, top, h, span, stuff)` (`tube`,
   `bars`, `rope`), `plankWall(x, top, w, color, line, ground)`, `panelDoor(door, color, panel)`, `post(stuff, x, top, bottom, w)`,
   `ladder`, `leg`, `rowboat`. Its material is a `Stuff` (three to five tones of one hue, darkest first): `WOOD`, `POST`, `TAR`,
   `CAST`, `STEEL`, `CONCRETE`, `ROPE`, `painted(color, line)`, or one of the level's own made the same way from the colours the
   level already paints.
3. **Otherwise draw it with `prop(kind, stuffs, key, w, h, x, y, paint)`** in the level's file: `paint` gets a grid and sets its
   pixels (`dot`, `box`, `tone(stuff, i)`) once; the piece is kept by its kind and key and copied every frame. Name every
   material it is drawn in (`stuffs`), so the test can hold them.
4. **Its tones**: no tone lighter than the paint for painted wood (a lighter one comes near his skin and the coins), none lighter
   than the lamp's hat for iron (a lighter grey comes near his helmet); a highlight is a pixel, not a band. `alive/props.test.ts`
   holds every level's props no nearer any role than its flat props were, or than 10, whichever is less, and none within 8 of the
   glamour's family, and every material in order 26's band. Add the level's old prop colours to its `OLD` and what it must draw
   to `EXPECTED` there.
5. **Cheap.** One copy a prop a frame; a long railing is a span copied along. Never texture a prop per frame in rectangles.
6. **Langkaia is in the first script**: it reaches the kit through `TEXTURED.prop` and `TEXTURED.walls` (`render/tiles.ts`), and
   draws each prop plainly (flat rectangles where it stands) if `feel`'s file never comes (order 30, ADR 0053: `TEXTURED.prop?.(…) ||
   R(…)`); any level fetched later imports the kit directly, and `feel`'s file with it.

What the invaders throw is theirs and is not a prop: each shot trails three pixels of its level's first neon behind it, fading
(`trails` in `render/feel.ts`): none once the shield has spent it, none inside the rune's ring, none with reduced motion. The
line map is drawn the same way: each station's skyline a piece of its own (`levels/kartArt.ts`), its piers, decks and station
marks textured. Before and after of anything redrawn: two builds served side by side, the same addresses taken at 1280 x 850
(four screen pixels an art pixel), and `tools/glance/diffcrop.py` crops both to what changed.

## What just happened

Order 19 (ADR 0040). Every event answers at once, where it happened, in its role's colour, with a sound, and with
a still answer under reduced motion. The answers are pictures and sounds only, in `src/render/feel.ts`; the rules
never wait for them. For anyone adding an event or a level:

1. **Answer within two frames, where it happened.** Nothing that changes the game's state is silent or unseen. Draw
   the answer at the thing (not at him, unless it is his), in its role's colour: a hit in the hurt red, a pickup in
   copper and gold, a boss in the "now" violet, a thing done with the tick, a thing to use in the use cyan.
2. **A hit is unmistakable.** He flashes the hurt red; the picture holds four steps (drawing only: the page draws
   nothing new while the rules step on, and the tests hold the two the same); the lost heart breaks in two in the
   header and the rest swell once; while he cannot be hurt he blinks a pale figure that reads after dark; at one
   heart left the last keeps a slow pulse (with reduced motion a still red ring).
3. **A stomp pays.** The enemy's glamour breaks (order 27): its glammed self flashes, its marks fly off in a burst of
   glitter in its level's neons, and it turns to its natural self in the calm outline and leaves; its skilling
   rises; his bounce kicks up a little dust. A stomp that does not kill says so: a hard white spark and two dry
   ticks, never a squash (a boss hit: a violet spark, the boss's sound, the hold).
4. **What he takes goes somewhere.** A skilling pops in a copper ring and the header's count swells; the cheese
   rises from where it lay and the lives count turns green and swells (the cheese is a life, not hearts: the
   bench is what fills hearts, one by one in the header); the bracteate rises with a glint and the gold count
   swells; the feather's landing glints with three high notes; the pole stands up beside him at Holmenkollen's
   rune; the shield's gift rings gold.
5. **What is about to happen is seen before it happens, in its outline.** Whatever gives way trembles first (ledges
   on a beat through their warning, pads and bundles and the sinking stone half a second before they go, the
   heron); give a level's own give-way ground `soon` in its `Grounds.stands` and draw it a `shiver` to and fro.
   A thrower's wind-up (a second) changes its outline by 18 pixels or more; a statue, a guard or a swan that
   changes its mind changes its outline (`feel.test.ts` holds both); a sledger about to set off leans in, and
   the ground where Gamlebyen's wall will close behind him trembles and throws up dust (order 20). A lift or a train about to leave flashes
   its stripe and rings; a crane hook clunks coming in to a landing and ding-dongs as it turns there. Never retime
   anything for a tell: draw it in the lead time that is there.
6. **A fall ends somewhere.** A splash in the water's colours where he went in, dust off a pit's edge, a ring
   closing on him where he is put back; the heart breaking in the header.
7. **Things done stay done in the picture.** A raised flag's tick pops in; a bench he has sat on keeps a tick; a
   cleared station's stone arrives with a flourish the first time only, and the station it opened answers.
8. **Reduced motion.** No shake, no hold, nothing flying, nothing blinking three times a second (the ledges'
   warning holds still); every event still has a still answer: a ring that stands, a tick, a held pose. A drawing that
   reads the clock to turn something on and off holds it with `view.reduced` on the same line (or reads `tt =
   view.reduced ? 0 : t`), or is an idle or a cycle named in `render/reduced.test.ts` with why (order 30).
9. **Cheap.** Forty particles at most, plain rectangles, from pools made once; nothing new made in a frame.
10. **A sound for every event**, from the kit's synth (`VOICES`, `SOUND_OF` in `feel.ts`), each its own; a hit, a
    stomp, a block and a dud stomp told apart with eyes shut (the test holds them apart by register, direction
    and noise). The front page's sounds do not change.

The events and what answers each, before and after, are in `tools/review/o19/` (`before.md`, `after.md`), with
frame strips made by `tools/glance/strips.ts` from `tools/glance/events.tsv`.

## In the hand

Order 21 (ADR 0043). On a touch screen (`(pointer: coarse)`; `?debug&touch` to look at one without it) the phone's
screen is the game's. The rules, each checked by `tools/glance/hand.ts` at 360 x 640, 390 x 844, 430 x 932, 844 x 390
and 932 x 430 at a pixel ratio of 3 (`tools/review/o21/`), and where a test can hold it, by a test:

1. **The game gets the screen.** Upright the picture gets at least 62 % of the height, on its side at least 78 %:
   nothing solid lies under it, and while a level is on (under 1024 x 600) the bar is the run alone (runes, clock,
   hearts, lives, skilling, sound), two rows upright, one thin row on its side. The name, the site's links, the gold
   count and the restart fold away (body class `playing`) and are whole again on the map and on a card.
2. **The help line is for keyboards.** On a touch screen it never names a key; it says only what changed (the pole,
   the shield, the sledge mounted or left) in the pad's words, for five seconds, in at most two lines at 360 wide,
   under the bar or just over the pad, wherever it covers neither him nor the ground ahead (`pad.test.ts`).
3. **The banner does not sit on the game.** A place's name shows on arrival and goes; it stands where it always stood
   or right under the bar, and if both would cover him, an enemy, something thrown or something to take, it has gone.
4. **Buttons fit thumbs.** Every target at least 56 x 56 CSS pixels with 8 between, inside the safe areas: the bar's
   buttons and links (their look at the top of the target), the cards' buttons, the pad's controls. Left and right
   are one wide pad in the bottom left corner that a thumb slides across; jump the biggest and lowest, in the bottom
   right corner; the dash to its left a little higher; one slot for up and down (Go in at a door, Sit at a bench,
   Shield, Duck on the sledge; Go in dimmed with nothing to do), over the move pad upright and over the jump on its
   side. A control with nothing to do now is dimmed, never taken away (`pad.test.ts`).
5. **A button answers the thumb.** Pressed shows in the event that pressed it; a finger that slides off lets go; a
   second finger never cancels the first; lifting one never lets go of the other's; turning the screen, losing focus
   or hiding the page lets go of everything (`pad.test.ts`, synthetic pointer events).
6. **The pad does not hide the game.** The buttons are see-through; he stands at most two thirds of the way down the
   screen above the pad's bottom row, and he, the two tiles under him and six ahead are never under a button: where
   they would be (near a level's ends) the camera eases up from a tile and a half before (`game/frame.ts`;
   `frame.test.ts` along every route at the five sizes, pixel ratios 2, 2.625 and 3, with and without a notch).
7. **Nothing else moves the page** while a level is on: no panning, zooming, double-tap, selection or callout, no
   300 ms delay. A card scrolls if it must and never goes under the bar.
8. **Turning the phone** lets go of every finger, lays everything out again, and the level goes on.
9. **The buttons are furniture**: greys and white, see-through, none of the roles' colours, nothing moving by itself
   (`pad.test.ts` reads the pad's styles).
