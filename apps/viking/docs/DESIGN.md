# Vikingfjorden: how it is built

A side-view game built the way the front page is built. The reasons (one cloth for all three games, the style bible) are in [`docs/games/PLAN.md`](../../../docs/games/PLAN.md), the levels with a picture of each in [`docs/games/LEVELS.html`](../../../docs/games/LEVELS.html), line 2's plan in [`docs/games/PLAN-LINE-2.md`](../../../docs/games/PLAN-LINE-2.md); this file says what is here.

The kit (`packages/pixelkit`) holds the map reader, the physics with moving ground, surfaces (ice, an inrun, a belt) and zones that push (`zone.ts`, on a beat for a gust), hazards (`hazard.ts`), the dash, carriers on a path (stopping at stations), an orbit or a lift, flags, cannons and drums, ledges on a beat and pulses (`timed.ts`), the dark with lights through it (`dark.ts`, which the maze's dark level uses too), the camera, doors, props and the scene helpers; and line 2's pieces: the rocking deck (`deck.ts`), the sinking pad (`sink.ts`) and the oar bank (`oars.ts`). Swatches used by one level alone (`swatches/metro.ts`, `hill.ts`, `winter.ts`, `museum.ts`, `park.ts`, `sledge.ts`, `lake.ts`, `site.ts`, `ruins.ts`) are imported by that level directly, so they come in its file.

## The rules

- **Movement**: the kit's physics with the front page's constants, 60 steps a second: run 1.6, a jump 46 px high and 54 px far. The **raven's feather** from the maze gives a second jump in the air (91 px high, 101 px far): on when `localStorage["viking.feather"] === "1"`, or with `?feather`. It is read again when he comes out of the mound. With `viking.featherGold === "1"` the pompom and the feather pixels are gold. `?feather` and `?pole` work without `?debug`, deliberately: anyone may try the game with the abilities (and keep a bracteate found so); it is a personal site, not a contest.
- **A level** starts at `S` and ends at its rune: the clock stops, the rune flies to its slot in the header, and a card shows the time, the best, the skilling, the bracteate and what is new. It has one action, "Til kartet" / "To the map": any key newly pressed, a tap or a click takes him there, and after six seconds it goes by itself (a thin line fills under the button; not under reduced motion, where it waits). He arrives at the station he cleared, the next one open ahead of him. Playing a level again is what the map is for; R in a level still restarts it. The Game over card keeps "Try again" (ADR 0037).
- **The header's runes** (order 18): a rune he has is a solid gold tile with the rune dark on it and a tick at its corner; one he has not is an empty slot, a faint dashed outline with the rune barely there; the level he is in is ringed. 27 x 33 on a desktop (21 x 30 with sixteen); on an upright phone the four links share one row, so the runes have a row of their own (30 x 34, sixteen 19 x 28) and the clock, hearts and the rest a row under them, all in view; with sixteen below 1400 px the best time is left to the card. The line map's rune stones speak the same language: gold with a tick once cleared, an empty dashed slot until then, both readable after dark.
- **Hearts and lives**: three hearts; an enemy's touch, something thrown, or a fall into water or a pit takes one, knocks him back and leaves him blinking and safe for a second and a half (with reduced motion he is drawn at half strength instead of blinking). A fall while he still blinks from a hit puts him back but takes no second heart (ADR 0018). Falls put him back at the last flag or bench (or the last firm ground where there is none yet). No hearts left: a life goes and he starts at the last flag or bench with full hearts. No lives left: a plain Game over card, and the level starts over with three lives; nothing cleared or found is lost. In a boss's fight (the boss awake) Game over goes on instead from the bench before it, with three lives and the boss whole and asleep again; the card says so (ADR 0017); and a single lost life there never puts him further back than that bench (ADR 0033). Any way back into a level after a Game over (the card, R, the header's restart, Back, a link) gives three lives. Lives and skilling go with him from level to level during a visit; 100 skilling or a slice of brown cheese is a life. A visit remembers, per level, that the cheese was taken and which skilling and beaten enemies have paid into the purse, so restarting a level farms nothing (ADR 0018).
- **Benches**: up at a bench to sit; the hearts fill and the bench is where falls bring him back.
- **Enemies** (in every level; how they look, see "Colour says what a thing is" below): walkers, flyers, throwers and jumpers are beaten by landing on them (higher with the jump held); spiky things hurt from above too; throwers lob on a beat after a one-second wind-up, and only while he is within seven tiles; jumpers leap out of water after a ripple. A beaten enemy puffs into squares and gives a skilling. Langkaia's pigeon on the quay is the first, on its own; Akershus's brazier on the ground before the outer wall is the first spiky thing, Rådhuset's chip-throwing gull the first thrower. A thrower may face one way only (`faces`: Bygdøy's slingers on the storehouses' ridges look out over the yard and throw only at him in front of them, not right under them) and may lead him (`lead`: it aims where he is going when he comes at it, and allows for the drop from above him): walking on under it is what it punishes, and the shield is the answer. Where they stand: no patrol within three tiles of a flag or a bench, no thrower within reach of a flag, a bench or a sign (tested); no patrol within three tiles of where a route's jump over water or a pit takes off or lands, whatever the move is called, and no flyer whose wave crosses the jump's arc (tested from the routes since order 13, the flyers and every move since order 17, with named exceptions: in Nabolaget the snowball kid at the edge of the block's roof and the cat at the foot of the drift, both stomped by the route first; in Østensjøvannet the three swans, whose water the pads lie on and which cannot be touched until he has lingered there; four of line 1's flyers, measured to cost the sloppy player little there); none by a drop, and nothing spiky on or beside ice (checked by the sloppy player, not by a test). After dusk (any sky but the day's) each enemy and what it throws gets a one-pixel pale rim and a little of its own light back through the night's tint, with eye-shine where it has eyes (the pixels it drew in `ENEMY.eye`); the hazards' warning stripe and the platforms' standing tops keep their light, and the skilling shine nearly as by day.
- **Surfaces**: ice has little grip, and the red rails stop a slide; an inrun has none at all, draws him on and lets his speed climb past a run; leaving the ground faster than a run keeps the speed in the air.
- **The shield** (given at Bygdøy's prow; ADR 0024): holding down on the ground raises it, facing the way he faces, in six steps, and it stays up as long as down is held. While it comes up and is up he cannot walk, jump or dash (a jump pressed then is kept a tenth of a second and made as down is let go, ADR 0032); he can turn, and it comes up again the other way. Up, it stops a throw coming at him from in front (faster across than 0.3 px a step towards him; it falls, harmless) and holds off a walker, a spiky thing, a flyer or a boss that runs into him from in front at his height, pushed back a tile (the plough then backs off to rest with the round's hint; the troll steps back and raises its club there, ADR 0032). It does not stop a fall, a stone dropping from above, anything from below (a jumper, the troll's wave), the troll's club, the third rail, or anything from behind. From the level after Bygdøy on he always has it, and everywhere once Bygdøy is cleared. `?shield` gives it for checking, as `?pole` does. On a touch screen the Shield button beside Dash, once he has it (with both, the pad's buttons are smaller). Down meant nothing in a level before it; up still sits at a bench. At Bygdøy's passage, where it is first asked for, its key shows over him ("↓ Shield") and the gift's bubble ends there. With reduced motion it is drawn down until it protects and then up.
- **The ski pole** (from clearing Holmenkollen): a dash of three tiles in ten steps, level, once each time in the air, and on the ground again after a short rest (24 steps). From Akerselva on he always has it, and some gaps need it. On a touch screen the Dash button; the sign and the card that teach it name the button there.
- **Zones** (Akerselva's falls): a rectangle that pushes him while he is in it. A waterfall pushes down: under it a jump barely leaves the ground, and walking off an edge into one drops him in its pool. Jump over a fall's top, or dash through it: the dash ignores the push.
- **The dark** (Nordmarka): under the spruces only his headlamp's circle shows, a little round him and more the way he faces; lit windows, the blue trail marks, the sign boards, the bogs' water and the dawn show through, and eyes shine in it. The marsh and the clearings are lighter. The headlamp lights what stands in front of the backdrop (trees, walls, the ground's top edge); where its circle falls on the sky, the far layers or deeper into the soil, the night is put back.
- **The troll** (Nordmarka's end, with a flag and a bench right before): asleep it is a boulder; it wakes when he comes in, walks at him, raises its club for a full second, slams it (a wave runs along the ground: jump it), and the club sticks for three seconds. Stuck where the dawn comes through the trees (a bright shaft with a pool of light on the floor, pulsing while the club is stuck) with his headlamp on it (seven tiles), a third of it turns to stone, the wave stops and it backs off, quickly, out of the light; three times, and it is a boulder with rune N on it. Its lines show in a bubble beside it, on the side away from him. A round with no hit shows a two-line hint over it and sends it back to the far end to come again; so does ten seconds of not being able to get nearer him. Its body hurts only while it walks or raises the club, not while the club is stuck, it flinches or it backs off. A lost life sends it back to sleep, its stone kept; Game over in the fight, see Hearts.
- **Flags** (checkpoints): touched once, the front page's flag goes up the pole, and falls bring him back there. Forwards only.
- **Moving ground** (carriers): rafts that bob or drift (two on Langkaia drift together and stand side by side), a rowing boat moored against the longship's stern (solid, so a step too far bumps into the hull), a ferry that crosses, lifts, a hoist, a bell that swings, banners on a path, logs that drift, lie a second against the rock at the end of their stretch and come round again, buckets on a water wheel and the tips of Rådhuset's clock hand (orbits), a moose's back. He is carried with them, and drawn on the same whole pixels as they are.
- **Cannons**: walk in, a third of a second in the barrel (the arc is shown, dotted), then the shot. In the air the keys only lean on the flight. A cannon he lands in takes him in: Akershus's saluting battery fires him from one to the next.
- **Ledges on a beat** (the bells): two seconds there, one second of the maze's warning shimmer, two gone. Neighbours are a step apart. Bygdøy's oars are ledges on a beat too, drawn as oars out of their ports (`oars.ts`), each a stroke behind the one below; an oar rings no bell (`note: -1`).
- **Rocking decks** (Bygdøy; `deck.ts`): a deck that tilts on a beat between two angles, a row of narrow ledges (4 px) that rise and fall in whole pixels, the ends most. Standing on it he is carried up and down with the ledge under his middle and slides nowhere; walking along it he is carried the pixel or two from one ledge to the next. Fram's far end must be up for a jump to reach her foredeck.
- **Sinking pads** (Bygdøy's bundles of balsa, Østensjøvannet's lily pads; `sink.ts`): firm for 40 steps under him (a lily pad 100: ADR 0029), then under over 30 (and he with it, into the water), back 90 steps after he leaves; stepping off before it sinks, it forgets him. The reach search takes every height a deck's ledge or a pad ever has, as it takes a log's.
- **A level's own rules** (`LevelDef.rules`, in the level's file, run by `play.ts` each step as it runs the troll's): its own enemies (by their look), ground of its own, the keys he is left with, an after-step hook, and for the reach search what carries him and what ground can ever be there; for the camera, how far it looks ahead on the sledge.
- **Statues** (Vigelandsparken): a statue walks toward him (0.8 px a step, half his run) while he faces away from it, and is stone while he faces it: still, harmless (it cannot be touched), in his way as a wall is, and its head is ground he can stand on. Looking is his facing, nothing more: a statue in front of him, or under him (his body over its body), is watched. It goes no further than its patrol, its ground and the walls allow. Granite, or bronze on the bridge; while it walks its eyes show and dust rises at its feet. The reach search takes a statue as a step anywhere along its walk. A wall three rows high is out of a jump's reach from the ground and in reach from a statue's head: the rose garden's statue is climbed onto the pergola, the courtyard's is led to the wall by turning his back on it. At the Wheel of Life the plinth is high and its step up a slab on a beat (two seconds there, one shaking, two and a half gone); he waits at its foot between a statue on the path behind him and one on the plinth ahead, and can face only one of them (since order 17).
- **The Angry Boy and the loose slab** (Vigelandsparken): a jumper high on his post; he stamps for a second, jumps and lands, and the slab over the bridge's broken span (a ledge on his beat) shakes while he is in the air and drops for 50 steps when he lands, then is back for 90.
- **The sledge** (Korketrekkeren; ADR 0026; mounting it, the help line comes back for five seconds to say down ducks, and on a touch screen the Shield button says Duck): from the top of the run to Midtstuen he sits on the sledge, and it carries him right at the stretch's speed (1.2 to 2.2 px a step, never over twice his run) whatever he presses: no walking, no stopping, no dash. He can jump, and duck (down, the shield's key: the shield stays packed, and the sign by the sledge says so; ducking he cannot jump). Ducking lowers his head by seven pixels against what hangs at head height: low branches, the crossing's barrier (a hazard on a beat, its lights blinking for a second before it comes down). A hit costs a heart and he rides on, blinking, with no knock-back. The run is drawn steep but is a stair of four-pixel ledges in the physics (the level's own moving ground); three snow bumps drop away and toss him up. Off the run (the gap) he is put back at the last flag, on the sledge. Sledgers are slower than he is, set off when he is ten tiles behind them, and pull off at the end of their stretch; landing on one is a stomp. On the sledge the camera looks 28 % of the view ahead, so he is drawn left of the middle; with reduced motion there are no speed lines and no shake.
- **Swans** (Østensjøvannet): a swan glides along its stretch of water and cannot be touched; once he has been in its stretch (over its water, on it or jumping over it) three seconds, it turns to him, lowers its neck and hisses, swimming nearer (drawn still with reduced motion); at four it comes at him along the water, its touch a heart, and it cannot be landed on. Leaving its stretch calms it at once, and so does catching him (it lets him be while he blinks). Each stretch is crossed pad to pad in about three and a half seconds.
- **The heron** (Østensjøvannet): a tall step, 28 px above the water, from the pad in the mist up to the second hide's deck; it flies off 60 steps after he lands on it, and is back three seconds later.
- **Throwers that face one way** may also be blind nearer than a tile and a half (`blind`: Bjørvika's riveters and Gamlebyen's archers, four tiles), since from above, leading a man close under them, their throw would come down too steeply for the shield.
- **Straight throws**: a thrower may throw straight and hard at him with no lob (`straight`: Bygdøy's passage slinger along the low passage, Østensjøvannet's gulls diving from their nests), aiming where they will meet when it leads (`lead`); a straight throw does not fall until the shield stops it. A thrower may see further than seven tiles (`reach`).
- **Crane hooks** (Bjørvika): a hook's block, two tiles wide, travels on a beat (`travel`) between two landings, slow at each end: at the near end it comes to a tile from the landing's edge, so on is a hop at the moment he picks; at the far end it comes right up to the next landing, and off is a step. Every landing over the drop is four tiles wide or more; the first hook crosses a bay with a safety net under it (a miss costs nothing; its step leads back up); on the traverse three hooks in a row meet a tile apart, the middle one swinging twice as quick as the other two (he goes for it as it comes in), each meeting the next every four seconds, with a gust of its own blowing across the traverse on a beat that is not the hooks'. A hoist (a lift) goes up the first tower's face; rebar sticks up from the floor (a hazard, lit at its edge after dusk), and on the third tower where a man who jumps off the hook instead of stepping off at its end comes down; a welder's sparks fall on a beat with a second's warning; loose sheets of steel slide across the shafts in the third tower's floor while gusts (Ekeberg's) push against him; riveters throw bolts along the girder, and two more along the jib's girder out from the roof, where the shield comes up twice or more. The level's beats are set so the scripted route meets each as it comes (`tools/review/tune-bjorvika.ts`).
- **Stones that rise** (Gamlebyen; ADR 0028): a ruin's missing stones (`Z` walls, `z` ledges, open on the map as written) rise when he comes within six tiles, in a third of a second, and stay: so the way forward shows itself as he goes. One that would rise where he stands waits until he has moved off. Twice a wall rises behind him once he is three tiles past it: from his side four rows high, from the side he came two (a jump), so a fall that puts him back before it never leaves him stuck. The water mirror's stepping stones are not alike (since order 17): no gap a full jump wide, wide stones, one that rises only from the edge of the one before, and one that sinks under him a second after he lands and comes up again once he has left it. The level's own ground changes the map it is played on (`Grounds.map`). The bishop's guards stand as statues (calm, harmless, not to be touched) until he comes within five tiles, then walk; a tower builds itself under him, a pixel a step, from the water mirror's far bank to the town wall's walk, and sinks back once he has left it three seconds. Torches light their surroundings after dark.
- **The sea serpent** (Gamlebyen's quay; ADR 0027, 0031): asleep until he passes its bench and the flag beside it; then the whole quay, from the planks' first board to the last longship's prow, is its fight. A ring of foam a second and a half where it will rise (at least four tiles from him, within seven where it can; if he walks up to the ring it moves once, with a fresh ring), it rises and rears for its tell (a second and a half, then a quarter less after each hit, never under a second), and spits a ball of brine straight at him at the height he last stood at (the quay or the prow). With the shield up and facing it the ball goes back and dazes it: its head lies on the quay's edge four seconds, and a stomp on it is a hit. Unblocked, or with his back to it, the ball costs a heart; it watches him two and a half seconds, then rests three. After a round its tail lashes along the quay from behind its neck towards him (its fin breaks the water a second before; the shield does not stop it; jump it) while its neck stays up. After its first hit it rises twice a round (the first ball sent back it ducks and comes up elsewhere with its own ring and tell; the second dazes it) and its tail comes along the quay after each ball, after its second hit closely. Its neck stands in his way while it is up and never hurts. If he goes back off its quay it sinks and waits, its hits kept. Three hits and it sinks; the water stills, the sky turns over five seconds to a dawn with the sun coming up behind the hills, the quay's torches go out one by one, and the rune rises out of the water to the last longship's prow (a carved dragon on its stem); only then can it be taken. Hints beside it, each only when true: as it first rises; "raise the shield while it rears" when its ball hit him facing it or went past him; "face it with the shield" when it hit his back; "jump on its head" two seconds into a daze; "its tail comes along the quay, jump it" as the tail hits him; and again after ten idle seconds, unless he has since done it. Game over in its fight goes on from its bench (ADR 0017).
- **The gold bracteate**: one per level, only reachable with the feather. Kept once found, counted in the header.
- **Belts** (Stortinget): the escalators and a walkway carry him along, or against him, as he walks.
- **Trains** (Stortinget): carriers on a timetable that stand at the platforms, five locals on the line, so the wait for one he can still get onto is never much over five seconds; a board counts down to the next. Their roofs are the only way through the tunnels; a low beam sweeps a rider off at the next platform. In the cavern the express passes over the local, which goes down to the depot.
- **Hazards** (Stortinget): the third rail in the open track costs a heart, as an enemy's touch does, and throws him clear, back the way he came.
- **Gusts** (Ekeberg): zones on a beat. Leaves stream through for a second (the warning), then for a second and a half it pushes. The first blows over flat ground with nothing else in it: it is the lesson. Sheep may stand on their knolls (`sheepRange`); three of them bounce him over the quarry.
- **The plough** (Nabolaget's end, with a flag and a bench right before): see `plough.ts`. Its cab is too high to jump onto from the street. It wakes in a second, comes at him, revs for a full second (its beacons flash and its blade lifts; with reduced motion both beacons burn with their glow), charges, and sticks in the bank (rounds one and two) or turns (round three). After a hit it backs off six tiles and comes again. A round with no hit shows that round's hint beside it: stuck or turned with no hit, run into him, or ten seconds of nothing; after running into him, or the ten seconds, it backs off to rest five seconds at the far end of its stretch, and while he leans on it there it does not set off, saying again every ten seconds how to beat it. Its body hurts only while it moves at him, driving or charging; revving in place, standing at the end of its stretch, stuck or turning it is harmless, and standing still it is in his way: he cannot walk through it. Its lines show in a bubble beside it, on the side away from him, never over its cab.
- **The ending**: with all ten runes the helmet turns gold (`viking.crowned` is set), the header's runes are gold, the card says so once, in both languages, and the line map has all ten stations open. With all six of line 2 the header's VIKING is gold too, and Gamlebyen's card says so and what is left to find (bracteates, better times); under the last station of a line whose every level has a best time, a board adds them up; with all sixteen, a longship lies at anchor at the end of the line past the last station's boards, its stern lantern lit after dusk, and lanterns stand along the viaducts (ADR 0030, 0031). Gamlebyen's own ending is the dawn above. The Pi's light in the yellow house's window is red until Nabolaget's rune is taken, then green.
- **Clicking or tapping** walks him there: on the map and the front page as the kit does it (hopping what is in the way); in a level carefully (`game/careful.ts`): never a jump, and he stops at an edge (water, a pit, any drop) or before an enemy rather than walk into it. The levels' help line does not suggest it.
- **The help line** at the foot of the page names the keys; it goes at his first move, and comes back for five seconds whenever what it says changes (the shield given, the sledge mounted or left, the pole the first time). Not on a touch screen, where the pad shows.
- **Loading**: a level's file is fetched when first needed; meanwhile the scene holds dark under a board "Toget til Nordmarka kommer … / The train to Nordmarka is coming …", and the level only opens if he is still waiting for it. If the fetch fails, a card says so plainly ("Innstilt / Cancelled": the train did not come), with "Try again" (the page again, at that level) and "To the map".
- **Upright phones**: the view is never narrower than 240 art pixels (the kit's `minWidth`, ADR 0019), wider than a thrower's usual reach (seven tiles) either side of him; a thrower given a longer reach (Bygdøy's passage slinger, twelve; Bjørvika's riveters, ten or twelve; Gamlebyen's archers, ten) can begin its wind-up just off such a screen's edge, its throw seen a second or more before it arrives. On a view taller than it is wide he stands about two thirds of the way down, and the bottom row of the map goes on down under him (ground, or deep water); the far layers are drawn as for a view of the usual height round him and moved down to where he stands, so they sit behind the street with the sky above them; under the ground (Stortinget, `underground`) the rock goes on up instead. The help line wraps on a narrow screen (ADR 0021).
- **The banner**: the place's name and landmark on a dark plate near the top, full from the first frame and fading after about two and a half seconds, in every level and on the line map, so it reads over any sky.
- **The line map's two lines** (ADR 0025): line 1 red on its viaduct, line 2 orange, leaving at Stortinget (an orange bar on its station mark), under line 1 on a low deck and up beside line 1's end onto its own viaduct with six stations. Once line 2 is open the map's banner names both lines, on arrival at a station too. Until Vålerenga is cleared a gate of boards stands across line 2 with a sign ("Opens once Vålerenga is cleared"), and all of line 2 is drawn faint. The header shows line 2's six rune slots once it is open; Vålerenga's card says when it opens.
- **Doors**: T-banen at each level's start goes back to the map; from the front page he arrives at Langkaia's start a step clear of Hjem, so his first up is a jump; Hjem goes to the front page (from Langkaia and the map to the Opera's door, from Nabolaget to the start of the front page); Runesmia (`/qr/`) and Verkstedet (GitHub) are sheds on Langkaia's quay, and Runesmia is also the smithy by the wheel at Hjula; Jernhaugen's mound in Nordmarka leads into the maze and back out to Nordmarka; the kiosk under Stortinget is Quintle's, padlocked with the front page's words until it is out. On the map, a station opens when the level before it is cleared.

## Colour says what a thing is

Order 18 (ADR 0039). Every thing on the screen has a role, every role has its colours, and a role's colours are
used for nothing else. The roles are named in `packages/pixelkit/src/swatches/roles.ts`; each role's colours are
in a file of their own; `src/render/palette.test.ts` holds them apart from each other and from every level's
fields, by day and under the evening, twilight and night tints (CIEDE2000, at least 10).

| Role | Colours | How it is drawn |
|---|---|---|
| Him | his tunic's blue, his skin, his iron helmet (`person.ts`) | No field of any level is near his blue by day; where one was (Ekeberg's fjord, city and ridge; Bjørvika's shore and glass; the deep water), the backdrop moved. |
| What can hurt him and moves (enemies, what they throw, the bosses) | `swatches/enemy.ts`: signal red, orange, magenta-plum, poison green, each with a lighter shade; near-black outline; a pale for eyes and one for teeth and claws | Drawn a quarter larger than before order 18, through `outlined` (`render/sprite.ts`): a one-pixel outline round the whole shape, painted once per shape and copied. The eyes it draws shine after dark. Stone does not bite: a watched statue, a guard still a statue, a pulled-off sledger, the troll asleep or stone, the plough parked are drawn plain. The bosses keep their looks, outlined; while they can hurt, their outline is the warning red. |
| What can hurt him and is not an enemy (rebar, the third rail, branches, the barrier, sparks) | the enemy palette's warning red, broken by its near-black | The same stripe along the dangerous edge, while it hurts (blinking through its warning). |
| What he can stand on | `swatches/stand.ts`: mint top, teal body (light, dark), dark line under; violet for what moves, lavender for what gives way | One-way ledges: teal, a mint top two pixels thick, a dark line under (`STAND_LEDGE`). Everything else he stands on gets the standing top over its own drawing (`standing` in `main.ts`): mint where it holds, mint over a violet stripe where it moves (every carrier, the rocking decks, the tower), lavender dashes where it gives way (ledges on a beat, sinking pads, lily pads, the heron, the stone that sinks). Crates and the level's own one-way tiles get the mint top. The tops keep their light after dark. |
| What he takes | copper and gold with a pale glint (`swatches/coin.ts`, the gold in `metal.ts`) | As before; the copper is darker and the glint paler (order 18). |
| What a boss shows before it acts, and where to hit it | one violet, `ROLES.now` | The troll's raised club, the edges of the dawn's pool while its club is stuck; the plough's blade and beacons while it revs; the serpent's ring of foam, its swelling throat, the stars over its dazed head. |
| Things done | a tick: a dark disc with a pale check | On an earned rune in the header and on the map, on a raised flag. |
| Where he falls | a dark band | Down the bank's face wherever ground stops over deep water or a pit with no floor. |
| Speech | the white bubbles, the dark plate | The kit's `text.ts`. |
| Scenery | everything else | Quieter than every role: lower in saturation and contrast. Ekeberg's Scream bands were toned down for it. |

**Adding a level, or a thing to one.** Draw what can hurt him only from `ENEMY`, with its eyes in `ENEMY.eye`, and
let the page outline it (a level's `art.enemy` is called inside `outlined`; wrap what is not its body, a nest or a
ripple, in `plain`). Make its picture's top where its box's top is (the test holds it within two pixels). Draw
what he can stand on so its top is where the physics has it: one-way letters get `STAND_LEDGE` by themselves,
and a level's own moving ground says what it does through `Grounds.stands` (holds, moves, gives way). Keep the
level's fields (anything that covers more than about one part in 250 of what it paints) off every role's
colours: `palette.test.ts` will say which and by how much.

## Checking

`bun test` plays each level with a scripted player (`src/game/bot.ts`) using the real rules: each must be cleared with the single jump, raising every flag, without losing a heart, and taking at least 40 s (Nabolaget 80 s; 6 to 11 with the ski pole, Bygdøy with the shield it gives; ADR 0020); with the feather each bracteate is reached. Every level is held to the order's measure: 220 to 280 columns, 40 to 80 skilling, eight to twenty enemies, one brown cheese, one or two benches, two to four signs, no enemy at a flag or a bench, no thrower in reach of a flag, a bench or a sign, and no enemy's patrol within three tiles of where a route's jump over a pit or water takes off or lands (the jumps played from the routes; exceptions by name in the test). A search with the real `stepBody` (`src/game/reach.ts`), over a world where every moving thing is everywhere at once, every ledge on a beat is always there and every enemy and sheep is a trampoline all along its patrol, with the ski pole's dash, proves each bracteate cannot be reached without the feather; zones push as they do in the level, gusts searched blowing and calm; standing on moving ground he may drop through it, as he does when it moves away; the ten searches run side by side in workers. The same search keeps its graph (which spot leads to which, and which moves need the dash), and the tests look in it for dead ends: no spot he can get to with what he is sure to have (the single jump; the pole from Akerselva on) from which neither the rune nor a fall that puts him back can be reached, nor one from which the rune can only be reached by falling (Holmenkollen's goal is the hut at the top of the inrun). `rules.test.ts` tests hearts, lives, pits, benches, skilling, the enemies, the drum, the dash, zones and the troll on small made-up levels, and hazards, gusts, sheep on a knoll, the plough and the shield. The kit's own tests cover the rocking deck, the sinking pad and the oar bank (`deck.test.ts`). Bygdøy's promises (the deck's high end, a bundle going under, the oars' stair, the shield given and used, the passage under the loft where a careless walker is always hit and a shielded one never, the waits) are in `levels.test.ts`, as are Vigelandsparken's (the walls a statue is for, the courtyard's statue led, the slab on the boy's beat, the jets' waits, the ring throwers), Korketrekkeren's (no stopping on the sledge, the stair of ledges, ducking, the gap and its flag, every obstacle in view 1.2 s ahead through the real camera at four widths, the riding time) and Østensjøvannet's (the stones between the first pads, the stretches' crossing times, the heron, the diving gulls).
